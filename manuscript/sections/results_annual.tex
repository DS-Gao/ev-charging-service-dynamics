\subsection{Facility--activity proportionality across time scales}
Annual activity is positively associated with facility scale. The two PPML specifications, which model a conditional arithmetic mean, estimate elasticities of \AnnualPosBeta\ [\AnnualPosLow, \AnnualPosHigh] on the positive sample and \AnnualZeroBeta\ [\AnnualZeroLow, \AnnualZeroHigh] when supported coded zeros are retained (Table~\ref{tab:annual_models}). The two-sided tests of unit elasticity give $p=\AnnualPosPOne$ and $p=\AnnualZeroPOne$, respectively, and both intervals lie below one. A 10\% facility difference corresponds to conditional mean activity differences of \AnnualPosTen\% and \AnnualZeroTen\%, holding the covariates and fixed effects constant. These PPML estimates are subproportional; a positive facility--activity association does not by itself imply proportional growth.

The log-OLS coefficient is \AnnualOLSBeta\ [\AnnualOLSLow, \AnnualOLSHigh]. Its zero-coefficient test rejects no association, whereas its unit-elasticity test gives $p=\AnnualOLSPOne$; the interval allows both subproportional and superproportional relationships on the conditional-log scale. This contrast reflects different estimands rather than a contradiction: OLS models the conditional mean of log activity, while PPML models a conditional arithmetic mean at observed weekly exposure. The positive PPML sample has 445 rows in 104 cities; the zero-inclusive sample has 523 rows in 112 cities, including 453 positive and 70 source-coded-zero rows, so the two PPML estimates are not a same-observation zero-inclusion experiment.

The spline tests ask a further question about curvature with facility scale. Joint $p$ values are \LogSplineP\ for log OLS and \MeanSplineP\ for PPML. They test whether the two added nonlinear basis terms are jointly zero; they do not test $\beta=0$ or $\beta=1$, and failure to reject does not establish global linearity. SI Table~\siref{tab:nonlinear_tests} and Figure~\siref{fig:si_annual} pair the curvature tests with local-elasticity curves and their pointwise uncertainty. A constant PPML elasticity below one is already subproportional on the original mean scale, whether or not it varies with facility size.
